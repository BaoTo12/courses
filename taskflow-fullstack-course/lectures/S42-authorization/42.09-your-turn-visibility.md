# Lecture 42.09 · 🎯 Your Turn: Admins See All Tasks, Users Only Their Own, Enforced in One Place

> **Section:** S42 · **Type:** 🎯 Your Turn · **Track:** ★ Core · **Time:** ~40 minutes
> Solution: [`solutions/S42-authorization/42.10-solution.md`](../../solutions/S42-authorization/42.10-solution.md)

---

After 42.05, alice can't open Bob's task 23, but it still **appears in her list**, in the status counts, and possibly on her dashboard.

## Requirements

1. The task list, the status counts, the dashboard (overdue, due soon) and `/tasks/latest` show a USER only **their own** tasks, and an ADMIN **all** tasks.
2. The rule ("admin → all, user → own") is written **once**, in `TaskService`. No controller, view or DAO call repeats it.
3. A controller can't bypass it: there's no public `TaskService` method that lists tasks without a caller.

**Tests:** alice's list has "Write quarterly report" but not "Bob: private salary review"; bob's list is the reverse, and `?q=quarterly` finds nothing for him; admin's list has both.

## Hints

- 📦 `TaskFilter` now has an `ownerId` component (null = every owner) and `withOwner(Long)`; `TaskDao.countByStatus(Long ownerId)` and `latestId(Long ownerId)` take one too.
- Who should set `ownerId`: the controller (from a request parameter?) or the service (from the caller)?
- Keep 42.05's `visible(id, caller)` next to your new method: together they're the whole policy.
- `/debug/stats` has no user (loopback-only). Give it a clearly named method instead of weakening the others.
