# Lecture 16.01 · The Store's Job: Hold State, Run the Reducer, Notify Subscribers

> **Section:** S16 · R3: The Store
> **Type:** 📖 Theory · **Track:** ★ Core · **Time:** ~15 minutes
> 🔗 Official companion reading: Redux **Fundamentals, Part 4** ("Store")

## 1. Three responsibilities, nothing more

The Redux store is surprisingly small. It:

1. **Holds** the current state.
2. **Runs** the root reducer when an action is dispatched, and saves the result.
3. **Notifies** subscribers after each dispatch.

That's all. It doesn't fetch data, doesn't know React, doesn't compare states, and doesn't decide who re-renders. Everything else is built **around** it:

| Need | Built with | Section |
|---|---|---|
| Logging, analytics, crash reports | **Middleware** (wraps `dispatch`) | 16.07–16.12 |
| DevTools, timing, extra store methods | **Enhancers** (wrap `createStore`) | 16.05, 16.06, 16.11 |
| Async logic (API calls) | Thunk **middleware** | S18 |
| Re-rendering only what changed | React-Redux (`useSelector`) | S17 |

## 2. What you'll build in this section

By the end of S16 you'll have written, by hand:
- `createMiniStore`: the three responsibilities in about 40 lines (16.03);
- an **enhancer** that times reducers and adds a store method (16.06);
- a **logger middleware** with redaction of sensitive fields (16.08);
- **`compose`** and **`applyMiddleware`** (16.09).

Each is tested against Redux's real implementation, then TaskFlow switches to Redux's versions in `src/app/store.ts`. Understanding these ~100 lines is what makes Redux feel simple instead of magic, and it's what the Fundamentals tutorial is designed to teach.

## 3. Summary

- The store holds state, runs the reducer, notifies subscribers.
- Middleware and enhancers extend it without changing its core.
